# SERVER GOVERNANCE & CONSTITUTION

**Horror Writers Guild**

**Version:** 1.0 Draft  
**Status:** Proposed founding constitution  
**Effective date:** To be set by ratification

## Preamble

The Horror Writers Guild is a Discord community for writers who read, discuss, draft, revise, and publish horror. The server exists to support rigorous creative work, candid but consensual critique, and a community in which members can take artistic risks without surrendering basic safety or dignity.

The server is not governed by the personal preference of its technical owner. It is governed by this Constitution, the Community Standards adopted under it, and an elected Council of Elders. Discord's Terms of Service, applicable law, and immediate safety needs take precedence where they conflict with server policy.

## Article I: Definitions and Member Rights

### Section 1. Definitions

- **Archivist:** The Founder or successor who holds Discord's Server Owner role and maintains the technical backend.
- **Council:** The elected Council of Elders.
- **Councilor:** A seated member of the Council.
- **Member in good standing:** A member with no active suspension, no pending removal proceeding, and no restriction on voting or candidacy imposed through the moderation process.
- **Eligible voter:** A member in good standing who has belonged to the server for at least 30 days when the relevant ballot opens.
- **Founding voter:** A member in good standing when the founding ratification ballot opens. The 30-day membership requirement does not apply to that one ballot.
- **Moderator:** A member appointed by the Council to apply Community Standards and protect the server in day-to-day operations.
- **Emergency action:** A temporary action needed to stop an active raid, account compromise, credible safety threat, or major technical failure.
- **Creative work:** Prose, poetry, scripts, criticism, reviews, visual or audio work, and creative promotional material shared, submitted, critiqued, performed, promoted, or otherwise represented through the server.
- **Generative AI:** A system that generates or materially transforms expressive text, images, audio, or other creative content in response to a prompt or other input. It does not include non-generative spelling, grammar, formatting, speech-to-text, or text-to-speech tools that do not generate or substantially rewrite expressive content.
- **Constitutional amendment:** A change to this document. All other approved policies are ordinary server rules.

### Section 2. Member Rights

Every member has the right to:

1. Participate in server life without harassment, discrimination, doxxing, retaliation, or coercion.
2. Receive clear notice of applicable rules and of material governance decisions.
3. Report misconduct privately and receive a good-faith response.
4. Receive notice and an opportunity to respond before ordinary disciplinary action, except where immediate safety requires temporary action first.
5. Appeal a material moderation action to a decision-maker who was not involved in the original decision.
6. Vote and seek office when eligible under this Constitution.
7. Decline critique or discussion of their work except where they have expressly requested it.

These rights do not create a right to access the server regardless of conduct, a right to another member's labor, or a right to publish material that violates Community Standards or Discord policy.

## Article II: The Archivist

### Section 1. Role and limits

The Archivist is the technical custodian of the server. The Archivist retains Discord's Server Owner role only because Discord assigns unique technical authority to that role. The role carries no legislative, judicial, or political veto over the Council.

### Section 2. Responsibilities

The Archivist is responsible for:

1. Server permissions, security settings, bot maintenance, backups, integrations, and subscription administration.
2. Carrying out valid Council decisions in a timely and neutral manner.
3. Maintaining least-privilege access to accounts, bots, billing, and recovery credentials.
4. Giving the Council a plain-language summary of recurring costs, material security changes, and technical limitations.

### Section 3. Technical constraints

If a Council decision cannot be implemented safely, legally, or within Discord's available controls, the Archivist must explain the constraint promptly and propose the nearest feasible implementation. The Council may revise its decision, but the Archivist may not use feasibility claims as a disguised veto.

### Section 4. Emergency authority

The Archivist may temporarily lock channels, alter permissions, remove malicious integrations, disable compromised accounts, or take comparable action only to contain an emergency. Within 24 hours, the Archivist must notify the Council of the action, its reason, and its scope. Emergency measures expire after 72 hours unless the Council ratifies, modifies, or extends them by vote.

### Section 5. Succession

The Archivist must maintain a written succession and recovery plan available to the Council. If the Archivist resigns, becomes inactive for 30 days without notice, or is unable to serve, the Council will designate a successor and begin Discord's ownership-transfer process. Until transfer is complete, the Council may appoint an interim technical administrator with only the permissions necessary to operate safely.

## Article III: The Council of Elders

The Council holds delegated responsibility, not elevated status. Councilors are stewards accountable to the members who elect them, the standards they enforce, and the public record they maintain.

### Section 1. Composition and terms

1. The Council consists of five elected Councilors.
2. Councilors serve six-month terms beginning after the January and July elections.
3. There are no term limits.
4. The Council elects a Chair from among its members at the beginning of each term. The Chair schedules meetings, publishes agendas and records, and has no extra vote.

### Section 2. Powers and duties

The Council:

1. Adopts and updates Community Standards and ordinary server rules.
2. Appoints, supervises, and removes Moderators.
3. Reviews appeals, member grievances, and disciplinary matters reserved to it by policy.
4. Oversees events, channels, server architecture, and material uses of server funds.
5. Publishes governance records while protecting private information.
6. Maintains the continuity and health of the community.

### Section 3. Meetings, quorum, and votes

1. The Council meets at least once per month and may meet asynchronously in a private Council channel.
2. Three Councilors constitute a quorum.
3. Ordinary Council actions require a simple majority of votes cast at a quorate meeting.
4. The Chair votes like any other Councilor. A tied motion fails.
5. Removal from office, permanent bans on appeal, constitutional amendments, and emergency extensions beyond 72 hours require at least four affirmative Council votes unless this Constitution states otherwise.

### Section 4. Records and transparency

The Council must maintain a private decision log. It must publish a monthly public summary of adopted rules, event decisions, moderator appointments, financial decisions, and anonymized moderation statistics. It must not publish identifying details of reports, appeals, or protected personal information without consent or a compelling safety reason.

### Section 5. Conflicts of interest

A Councilor or Moderator must disclose and recuse from any matter involving themselves; a partner, family member, or close friend; a current or recent collaborator; or any other relationship that reasonably calls impartiality into question. The remaining eligible Councilors decide disputed recusals by majority vote.

### Section 6. Financial stewardship

1. The Council must approve any recurring expense, fundraising effort, paid partnership, or commitment of server funds above a threshold set in an ordinary rule.
2. The Council must publish a quarterly summary of money received, material expenses, reimbursements, and the current balance, without disclosing protected personal information.
3. No Councilor, Moderator, or Archivist may approve their own reimbursement, payment, or financial benefit. Disinterested Councilors must approve it in advance where practical, or promptly review it when an emergency makes advance approval impossible.
4. Server funds may be used only for the server's stated mission, ordinary operation, and member-approved activities. They may not be distributed as profit or personal compensation unless a disclosed, Council-approved agreement expressly authorizes payment for defined work.

### Section 7. Moderators

Moderators are appointed to apply the Community Standards consistently, protect members, and support the Council's work. The Council must set written selection criteria, training expectations, access permissions, and a regular performance-review process in ordinary rules. Moderators must document material actions, recuse from matters involving themselves, and may not use their role to obtain preferential access, promotion, or commercial advantage.

## Article IV: Elections

Elections are an opportunity to choose members for service, judgment, and accountable work. They are not a popularity contest, a vehicle for faction-building, or permission for personal attacks.

### Section 1. Eligibility

Any member in good standing who has belonged to the server for at least 30 days may nominate themselves or, with consent, another eligible member. Candidates must disclose any current moderator role, financial relationship with the server, or other material conflict relevant to the office.

### Section 2. Election calendar

Elections occur in January and July according to this schedule:

1. Nominations remain open for seven days.
2. A temporary `#election-booth` opens for seven days for candidate statements and member questions.
3. Anonymous voting remains open for 72 hours.
4. Results are published within 48 hours after voting closes.
5. The new Council assumes office within seven days after results are published.

### Section 3. Ballot and result

1. Elections use an anonymous ranked-choice ballot and single transferable vote count for the five Council seats. The Council must publish the counting method and ballot instructions before nominations open.
2. A tie that affects the final seat is resolved by a 48-hour anonymous runoff between the tied candidates.
3. The Council must publish the final count, participation total, and a plain-language explanation of how seats were allocated, but not identifiable ballots.
4. The Archivist or a neutral election administrator must preserve reasonable evidence of the result long enough to resolve a timely challenge.

### Section 4. Election challenges

A member may challenge an election within seven days by submitting evidence of a material procedural error, duplicate voting, fraud, or ineligible candidacy. Disinterested outgoing Councilors review the challenge. If fewer than three disinterested Councilors remain, a temporary three-member review panel selected by lot from eligible non-candidates will decide it.

## Article V: Vacancies, Removal, and Recall

Vacancy, removal, and recall procedures protect continuity and member trust. They are safeguards for serious failures of conduct or responsibility, not tools for relitigating ordinary disagreements or punishing unpopular but good-faith decisions.

### Section 1. Vacancies

A Council seat is vacant upon resignation, removal, death, permanent departure from the server, or 30 consecutive days of unannounced inactivity. The highest-polling eligible non-winner from the prior election may fill the seat for the remainder of the term. If no eligible runner-up accepts, the Council holds a special election within 21 days.

### Section 2. Removal from office

A Councilor may be removed for serious or repeated rule violations, abuse of authority, material nondisclosure of a conflict, or defined inactivity. The Councilor must receive written notice of the allegation and a reasonable opportunity to respond before a vote, except for temporary safety restrictions.

Removal requires either:

1. At least two-thirds of the disinterested Councilors, rounded up, with no fewer than three affirmative votes; or
2. A recall petition signed by at least two-thirds of eligible voters, followed by a simple-majority recall vote open for 72 hours.

If recusals or vacancies leave fewer than three disinterested Councilors, a temporary three-member review panel selected by lot from eligible members who are not parties, witnesses, or close associates of the parties decides the matter by a two-thirds vote.

### Section 3. Recall procedure

1. A recall petition must identify the Councilor, state the grounds for recall, and be submitted within 30 days after circulation begins.
2. A neutral election administrator, or a temporary review panel if the Council cannot act impartially, verifies eligibility and signatures while keeping signers' identities confidential except as necessary to investigate fraud.
3. Once verified, the Council must open a seven-day information period in which the petitioners and Councilor may make statements under the ordinary election conduct rules. The resulting recall ballot opens within seven days.
4. A failed recall petition may not be renewed against the same Councilor for 90 days unless materially new evidence arises.

### Section 4. Moderator removal

The Council may remove a Moderator by simple majority. An individual Councilor may temporarily suspend a Moderator's permissions during an emergency, subject to Council review within 72 hours.

## Article VI: Community Standards

### Section 1. Core conduct

Members must not:

1. Harass, threaten, stalk, discriminate against, or retaliate against another person.
2. Dox, share private communications without permission, or solicit personal information for coercive purposes.
3. Post illegal content, credible threats, sexual content involving minors, or material prohibited by Discord's Terms of Service.
4. Plagiarize, knowingly misrepresent authorship, impersonate another person, or use the server to run scams.
5. Disrupt workshops, flood channels, evade moderation, or recruit members into off-server disputes.
6. Deliberately or repeatedly misgender, deadname, or deny a member's stated identity after being corrected.
7. Use the server for unsolicited commercial solicitation, referral marketing, paid services, crowdfunding, or recruitment into unrelated communities.

Members may share their own publications, paid services, crowdfunding campaigns, calls for submissions, and relevant professional opportunities only in Council-designated channels and in accordance with channel-specific frequency and disclosure rules. Direct-message solicitation based on server membership is prohibited unless the recipient has explicitly invited it.

### Section 2. Horror content and critique

Horror is broad. It may be supernatural or realistic, grotesque or quiet, intimate or cosmic; it may confront violence, grief, sex, death, trauma, taboo, and the unknown. This server is for adults who can engage such material with artistic seriousness, distinguish depiction from endorsement, use warnings and consent, and respect another member's limits. It is not a venue for shock-seeking, real-world cruelty, or demands for unrestricted access to disturbing material.

1. Horror, disturbing themes, and difficult craft discussions are welcome when posted in the appropriate channel and labeled with relevant content warnings.
2. Real-world graphic imagery, targeted bigotry, sexual violence presented for shock, and content that creates a credible risk to members may be restricted or removed.
3. Critique must be invited, directed at the work rather than the writer, and given in good faith. Members must respect a writer's stated feedback boundaries.
4. The Council may establish channel-specific limits for violence, sexuality, spoilers, and research material.

### Section 3. Age and adult material

The server is for adults aged 18 or older. Members must not post sexual or otherwise restricted material outside age-appropriate, access-controlled channels approved by the Council. No member may post or request sexual material involving a minor.

### Section 4. Authorship and AI-assisted work

Members must not present another person's work as their own. Generative AI may not be used to create, develop, revise, transform, or provide critique of a creative work shared, submitted, critiqued, performed, promoted, or otherwise represented through the server. This includes generated prose, imagery, audio, plot or character development, substantive revision, and AI-generated feedback. Non-generative accessibility and basic proofreading tools defined in Article I are permitted.

### Section 5. Workshop confidentiality and creative rights

1. A draft, critique, workshop discussion, or research material shared in the server remains the property of its creator or rights holder. Sharing it grants other members only the limited permission needed to read, discuss, and critique it within the stated channel or activity.
2. Members must not copy, repost, distribute, publish, adapt, submit, or use another member's shared work outside the server without the rights holder's express permission.
3. Members must not use server-shared drafts, critique, or workshop discussion to train, evaluate, or improve an AI system without the express permission of every affected rights holder.
4. Quoting a brief excerpt for a report, appeal, or moderation record is permitted only when reasonably necessary and subject to Article VII's confidentiality rules.

## Article VII: Moderation and Due Process

Moderation exists to protect members and the community's creative work. It must be timely when safety requires action, fair to the people affected, proportionate to the conduct, attentive to privacy, and open to good-faith reporting and appeal.

### Section 1. Reporting

Members may report misconduct, appeal a moderation action, or propose a change through `#contact-the-council` and its private ticket system. Reports should receive an acknowledgment within 72 hours, except where technical failure or an emergency prevents it. The Council may designate trained Moderators to receive reports. A report concerning a Moderator, Councilor, or the Archivist must have a private route that does not include the person reported.

### Section 2. Urgent reports and updates

1. Reports of an active raid, account compromise, credible threat, doxxing, or imminent risk must be marked urgent and may be acted upon immediately under Articles II and VII.
2. For a non-urgent report, the decision-maker must provide a substantive status update within 14 days unless doing so would compromise safety, privacy, or a live investigation. If no decision has been reached, the update must state the next expected review date.
3. The server cannot provide emergency services. Where there is an immediate danger to life or safety, members should contact local emergency services or the relevant platform reporting channel.

### Section 3. Confidentiality

Reports are shared only with people who need the information to investigate, protect members, or comply with Discord policy or law. Confidentiality cannot be absolute: it may yield to an imminent safety threat, platform reporting obligations, or a fair appeal process. Retaliation for making a good-faith report is prohibited.

### Section 4. Investigation and decision

1. Decision-makers gather relevant evidence, allow the affected member a reasonable opportunity to respond, and recuse for conflicts.
2. The response should be proportionate to the conduct, prior history, risk, and impact on the community.
3. Ordinary actions may include guidance, a warning, channel restriction, temporary timeout, suspension, removal from a role, or ban.
4. A Moderator may act immediately to contain active harm, but must document the action for Council review within 72 hours.

### Section 5. Appeals

Members may appeal a suspension of seven days or longer, a permanent role removal, or a ban within 14 days. Three disinterested Councilors decide an appeal. They may affirm, modify, reverse, or remand the decision. An appeal is not a rehearing merely because the member disagrees with the outcome; it must identify new evidence, procedural error, disproportionality, or conflict of interest.

### Section 6. Commercial Disclosure and Conflicts

1. A commercial interest includes payment, commission, referral benefit, ownership, employment, or any other material benefit connected to a service, publication, campaign, event, or opportunity promoted in the server.

2. Members must clearly label commercial posts in designated channels. Unsolicited commercial direct messages to members are prohibited unless the recipient has explicitly invited them.

3. Councilors and Moderators must disclose any commercial interest before promoting, endorsing, approving, or materially influencing discussion of a related service, publication, campaign, event, or opportunity.

4. A Councilor or Moderator with such an interest must recuse from related moderation, channel-access, funding, partnership, or disciplinary decisions.

5. Failure to disclose a material commercial interest may be reported and addressed under this Article's investigation, decision, and appeal procedures.

6. Disclosure does not authorize conduct otherwise prohibited by the Community Standards.

### Section 7. Records and retention

The Council must adopt a written retention schedule for reports, evidence, decisions, appeals, and election records. Records must be kept no longer than reasonably necessary for safety, accountability, legal obligations, and recurring misconduct review; access must be limited to those with a defined need. Members may request correction of factual errors in a record, though the Council may retain an annotated original where necessary for integrity or safety.

## Article VIII: Amendments and Ordinary Rules

This Constitution should be stable enough to protect members from impulsive governance and revisable enough to meet genuine new needs. Neither temporary conflict nor institutional inertia is a sufficient reason to evade its amendment process.

### Section 1. Ordinary rules

The Council may adopt ordinary server rules by simple majority after providing at least seven days of public notice and an opportunity for member comment, unless an emergency requires a temporary rule first.

### Section 2. Constitutional amendments

1. A proposed constitutional amendment must be published for at least 14 days of member comment.
2. Adoption requires at least four affirmative Council votes and a simple majority of participating eligible voters in a 72-hour ratification vote.
3. That ballot must include participation by at least 10 percent of eligible voters, rounded up, with a minimum of 10 voters. If fewer than 10 eligible voters exist, participation by a majority of all eligible voters is required instead.
4. Amendments take effect seven days after ratification unless the amendment states a later date.

### Section 3. Interpretation

The Council interprets this Constitution in good faith, favoring member safety, due process, transparent governance, and the server's creative mission. Interpretation may not be used to evade the amendment process.

## Article IX: Adoption and Revision History

This Constitution takes effect only after it is published for at least 14 days and ratified by a simple majority of participating eligible voters in a 72-hour vote. The first ratified version must record its effective date below.

For the founding ratification only, "eligible voters" means Founding voters. Founding ratification requires participation by 10 Founding voters or one-third of all Founding voters, rounded up, whichever is lower. If that threshold is not met, the Council must extend or rerun the process after additional notice rather than treating the proposal as adopted.

### Section 1. Dissolution

1. Dissolution requires at least 30 days of public notice, four affirmative Council votes, and approval by two-thirds of participating eligible voters in a 72-hour ballot that meets the same participation threshold as a constitutional amendment.
2. Before dissolution takes effect, the Council must publish a closure plan covering member notice, transfer or cancellation of services, disposition of funds, deletion or retention of records, and any feasible export of members' own content.
3. Remaining funds must first satisfy valid server obligations. Any remainder must be donated to a mission-aligned nonprofit chosen through the dissolution vote, unless legal or contractual obligations require another disposition. No officer may receive a distribution because of their office.

## Community Commitment

This server treats horror as serious art and community as shared stewardship. The Council, Archivist, Moderators, and members all hold different responsibilities, but none are above the standards that make the space worth building.
