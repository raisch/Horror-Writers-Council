# Member Guide: How This Server Works

This is a community for adults who write, read, discuss, draft, revise, and publish horror. It is built for serious creative exchange: thoughtful conversation, consensual critique, and shared support for the work of writing.

This guide explains how the community is governed in plain language. The [Server Governance & Constitution](./SERVER%20GOVERNANCE%20%26%20CONSTITUTION.md) is the controlling document if this guide and the Constitution differ.

## Who Can Participate

The server is for people aged 18 or older who want to engage horror as an art form and participate in good faith. Horror here is broad: supernatural or realistic, grotesque or quiet, intimate or cosmic. Difficult subject matter can be discussed when it is placed in the right channel, appropriately warned, and approached with respect for other members' limits.

Members may read, discuss craft, share opportunities in designated spaces, join events, offer critique, and submit their own work where the relevant channel permits it. You do not need to have published work to participate. You do need to follow the Community Standards, respect feedback boundaries, and avoid treating other members' work or attention as something you are owed.

The server is not a venue for harassment, bigotry, doxxing, scams, plagiarism, shock-seeking, unwanted sales pitches, or off-server conflict. Sexual content involving minors is prohibited. Deliberate or repeated misgendering, deadnaming, or denial of a member's stated identity after correction is also prohibited.

## Your Rights and Responsibilities

Every member has the right to safety, clear rules, a private route to report misconduct, notice and an opportunity to respond to ordinary moderation, and an appeal of material moderation decisions. Eligible members may vote and run for Council.

Creative work remains with its creator. Sharing a draft gives other members permission to read and discuss it in the stated activity, not to repost it, distribute it, adapt it, publish it, or use it outside the server. Do not use generative AI to create, develop, revise, transform, or critique creative work shared or represented through the server. Do not put another member's draft, critique, workshop discussion, or private communication into an AI system.

Critique is opt-in and human-to-human. Keep it directed at the work, follow the writer's stated boundaries, and give it in good faith. Non-generative accessibility and basic proofreading tools remain permitted when they do not generate or substantially rewrite expressive content. Use content warnings and the appropriate channels for disturbing material.

## How the Server Is Governed

The server is not controlled by the personal preferences of its technical owner. It is governed by the Constitution, Community Standards, and an elected five-member Council.

The **Archivist** holds Discord's technical Server Owner role and manages security, permissions, bots, backups, and technical recovery. That technical role does not carry a political veto over the Council. The Archivist may act quickly during an emergency, but must report the action to the Council, and emergency measures expire unless the Council reviews them.

The **Council** is elected by members to steward the community. It adopts ordinary rules, appoints and supervises Moderators, oversees events and finances, hears appeals, and publishes regular summaries of its work. Councilors serve six-month terms. Elections occur in January and July using anonymous ranked-choice ballots and a single transferable vote count.

**Moderators** handle day-to-day application of Community Standards. They are appointed and supervised by the Council, must document material actions, and must step aside from matters in which they have a conflict of interest.

Members in good standing who have been in the server for at least 30 days may vote and stand for Council, subject to the Constitution's election rules. The founding ratification uses a separate founding-voter rule because a new server will not yet have 30-day members.

## Conflicts of Interest and Money

Councilors and Moderators must disclose and step back from decisions involving themselves, close relationships, recent collaborators, or commercial interests. They may not use a role for preferential promotion or private benefit.

Commercial posts belong only in designated channels and must be clearly labeled. Uninvited commercial direct messages based on server membership are not allowed. The Council must publish financial summaries and cannot approve an officer's own reimbursement or payment without disinterested review.

## When Conflict Happens

Start with the least escalatory option that is safe. A misunderstanding about feedback may be resolved by clarifying a boundary or disengaging. Conduct that threatens safety, privacy, dignity, or the community should be reported.

Use `#contact-the-council` and its private ticket system to report misconduct, appeal a moderation action, or raise a governance concern. Reports should be acknowledged within 72 hours. Reports involving a Moderator, Councilor, or the Archivist must be routed so that the person reported does not handle them.

For raids, compromised accounts, credible threats, doxxing, or imminent danger, mark the report urgent. The Archivist or Moderators may take immediate temporary action to stop active harm. The server is not an emergency service; where there is immediate danger, contact local emergency services or the relevant platform reporting channel.

For ordinary reports, decision-makers gather relevant information, give the affected member a reasonable opportunity to respond, and choose a proportionate response. Outcomes may range from guidance or a warning to channel restrictions, a timeout, suspension, role removal, or a ban. Reports are kept private except where sharing is necessary to investigate, protect people, meet a platform or legal obligation, or conduct a fair appeal.

Suspensions of seven days or longer, permanent role removals, and bans may be appealed within 14 days. Three Councilors who were not involved in the original decision review the appeal. Appeals can correct a procedural error, new evidence, disproportionate outcome, or conflict of interest; they are not simply a second debate over a decision someone dislikes.

## How Rules Change

The Council can make ordinary channel and operating rules after public notice and a chance for member comment. The Constitution itself requires more: at least 14 days of comment, approval by four Councilors, a majority vote of participating eligible members, and a participation threshold.

Members can also hold Councilors accountable. Serious misconduct, abuse of authority, undisclosed conflicts, or sustained unexplained inactivity can lead to removal under the Constitution. Members may initiate a recall process, which has confidential signature verification, a structured information period, and a member vote.

## What to Do Next

Read the Community Standards before participating in workshops or mature-content channels. Ask questions about process in the governance channel, use the private ticket route for personal or safety-sensitive concerns, and raise proposed rules or amendments through the published governance process.
